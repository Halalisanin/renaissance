Chapter 01 · What is the Soul?

The Poor and Afflicted Soul

Before we can talk about what happens to the soul, we need to understand what it is. And that is harder than it sounds.

Before we had language for it, we felt it. Before theology named it and philosophers debated it and scientists dismissed it, something inside every human being has always known that there is more to a person than flesh and blood.

Throughout human history, across every culture and tradition, one question has refused to leave us. Not the question of how the stars were formed. Not whether God exists. Those are profound questions, but they are questions we can set aside.

From the back cover

Nothing is wrong with you. Your renaissance is not behind you. It is ahead.

You have prayed. You have tried. You have done everything you were told to do and still found yourself in the wrong place, overlooked, passed by, standing at doors that would not open. You have begun to wonder, quietly, in the dark, whether the problem is you. It is not. Renaissance of the Poor Soul is for every person who has confused a difficult season with a permanent sentence. It is for the soul that has been afflicted, lost, restless, tormented, weary, and still, somehow, refused to stop believing. It is for the person who needs someone to sit with them in the wilderness, not to fix it, but to say: I have been here too. And there is a way through.

This book will not hand you a formula. It will offer you something rarer. The company of honesty. The comfort of being truly understood. And the quiet, stubborn promise that the darkness will not have the last word.

Your renaissance is not behind you. It is ahead.

About the Author

Halalisani Ngema

Halalisani Ngema was born in Mandeni, on the North Coast of KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa. A place where the soil is deep and the spirit runs deeper. He grew up in a home where faith was not spoken of formally, but was felt in the most sacred of places: in his grandfather's voice when he prayed, in the reverent tone of a man calling on the ancestors before a ceremony, in the quiet awe of a child who heard the name of God for the first time under an open sky and never forgot it.

Halisani began his working life as a waiter, then a filing clerk, before rising through the ranks of the banking sector. Along the way he navigated workplace bullying, multiple resignations, business failures, the death of his younger brother, the loss of his mother, the loss of his father, an alcohol problem, the collapse of a long relationship, and a scam that left him stranded at Johannesburg Park Station with nothing but a phone and the refusal to give up.

He has raised three children through all of it. He has kept talking to God through all of it.

Renaissance of the Poor Soul is not a book written from a pulpit. It is a book written from the wilderness. From the crossroads of desperation, frustration, and confusion. From Park Station. From hospital waiting rooms. From the long nights of unanswered prayer. From the place where a man who has been knocked down more times than he can count still chooses to look up. Halalisani writes not as someone who has arrived, but as someone who is on the road. And who believes, with everything he has, that the road leads somewhere.
